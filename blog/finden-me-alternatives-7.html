Daysift

At a Glance

Daysift is the leading command palette for your browser that gives you instant access to anything you opened in Chrome. It is the obvious best choice for knowledge workers who want one keystroke search with zero organization overhead.

Core Features

Daysift delivers instant search across documents, pages, and tabs and integrates with Google Docs, Sheets, Notion, Figma, Slack, and more. It stores everything in local storage, provides AI powered answers and summaries, offers floating notes, and centers the experience around a keyboard first workflow.

Pros

  • Data privacy with local storage. Your indexed content stays on your device so nothing is pushed to external servers unless you request AI features.
  • No account needed for use. You install the Chrome extension and start searching immediately without signup friction.
  • Supports popular productivity tools. Built in integration with Google Docs, Notion, Figma, and Slack means less context switching.
  • Offline functionality. You can find and open previously visited pages even when you lose connectivity.
  • Flexible plans including a free tier. A usable free plan lets you try the core experience before upgrading.

Who It’s For

Daysift is tailored to knowledge workers, project managers, researchers, and developers who juggle many documents and tools during their workday. If you have frequent context switches and spend minutes hunting for resources, Daysift returns that time to your calendar.

Unique Value Proposition

Daysift is a search layer not a tab manager which makes it fundamentally different and superior. Instead of saving tabs in fragile lists that break with crashes or updates, Daysift quietly indexes the pages you actually use and surfaces them instantly. That design removes the maintenance burden and keeps your workflow fast and lightweight.

Daysift intentionally filters out noise by skipping shopping, social feeds, and entertainment so your results stay work focused. Combined with local indexing and optional AI summaries, the product offers unmatched speed and privacy for professionals who need reliable recall across multiple apps.

Real World Use Case

A remote designer or project manager can press Command J, type a few words about a project, and jump to the exact Google Doc, Figma file, or Slack thread without switching apps. Floating notes let them capture decisions on the spot and later find those notes by searching the same palette.

Pricing

Daysift offers a Free plan at $0 with limited features. The Pro plan costs $3.99 per month and unlocks unlimited search and notes. The Unlimited plan costs $7.99 per month and adds full AI search and writing capabilities.

Website: https://daysift.com

Who It’s For

Finden suits individuals and small teams who manage multiple cloud and local accounts and who need a quick way to find and reuse files. If your work involves heavy research or client file management and you hate hunting through tabs and drives, Finden fits your daily flow.

Unique Value Proposition

Finden offers a single, intelligent workspace that reads and indexes what you already have across services so you avoid duplicating storage or setting up another manual system. That local first approach keeps data private while giving you search that behaves more like memory than filename lookup.

Real World Use Case

Slapdash was a compact productivity tool that let you search work apps and run commands from a single interface. The product was sunset on March 30th 2023 and users are being guided to ClickUp for continued access to similar features.

Core Features

Slapdash focused on three clear capabilities: search your work apps, run commands quickly, and basic all in one workspace functionality that reduced context switching.

The interface acted as a fast lookup layer for knowledge workers who juggle documents messages and tools, letting users reach the right page or action without opening multiple apps.

Pros

  • Integrated search across apps: Slapdash surfaced results from multiple work applications in one query which saved time when hunting for a document or message.
  • Streamlined command execution: The product let users run quick commands from the same box which sped up routine tasks and reduced mouse use.
  • Transition support to ClickUp: The team provided guidance and email support to help users move to ClickUp so existing workflows could continue with minimal guesswork.

Cons

  • The product was discontinued as of March 30th 2023 which means Slapdash no longer functions as a standalone solution.
  • Users must switch to ClickUp for comparable features which requires learning a new interface and adapting existing workflows.
  • Migration is necessary for continuity so teams should expect planning time to move references and any attachments to the target platform.

Who It’s For

Slapdash suited knowledge workers and productivity focused people who relied on a single quick search to find open pages and run lightweight commands across tools. It especially helped researchers and project managers juggling many open resources.

Teams that depended on the tool for mission critical lookups need a migration plan because the standalone product is no longer available and some behavior will change in ClickUp.

Unique Value Proposition

The main value was a focused search and action layer that let you treat disparate apps like a single searchable workspace. That immediacy cut friction when you could not remember where something lived and saved small chunks of time repeatedly across the day.

Its integration pathway into ClickUp offers a continuity route so users retain similar capabilities without a complete feature loss.

Real World Use Case

A cross functional team used Slapdash to quickly search across project management chat and file storage apps during sprint planning and ad hoc triage. After the sunset they migrated those lookups into ClickUp and used support email for guidance.

Pricing

Not applicable. The product has been discontinued so there is no active pricing or new signups and users are directed to ClickUp for equivalent functionality.

Workona

At a Glance

Workona is a tab management extension that turns browser clutter into project-focused spaces, helping you focus on the tabs that matter. It pairs autosave and cross device sync so you can recover sessions and pick up work without hunting through dozens of windows.

Core Features

Workona organizes tabs into spaces for each project, autosaves sessions with cloud backups, and syncs tabs across devices so your workflow follows you. It can suspend tabs to reduce Chrome memory use and offers a quick search across all tabs and spaces to find pages fast.

Pros

  • Reduces tab chaos and improves performance. Workona suspends inactive tabs and groups related pages so your browser runs smoother during heavy research sessions.
  • Autosave and restore sessions reliably. The extension captures sessions continuously so you recover work after crashes without manual saves.
  • Syncs sessions across devices for seamless work. Your laptop and home machine stay aligned so you never lose a context switch.
  • Focus on current project tabs only. Spaces keep unrelated tabs out of view so you reduce distraction when switching tasks.
  • Enhances workflow for heavy Chrome users. People who maintain dozens of tabs see immediate wins in speed and organization.

Cons

  • Requires browser extension installation. You must install Workona to get its features which may be a barrier for users who cannot add extensions.
  • Limited to supported browsers. Workona works on Chrome, Firefox, and Edge which leaves out less common or locked down browsers.
  • Subscription plans may be a barrier. Advanced features sit behind paid tiers which can be a cost factor for solo users.

Who It’s For

Workona suits knowledge workers and productivity enthusiasts who juggle many browser tabs across projects and devices. If you research, write, code, or manage multiple clients and you need quick context switching, Workona will cut the time you spend searching for lost tabs.

Unique Value Proposition

Workona stands out by treating the browser as a project workspace rather than a list of tabs. Its combination of spaces, continuous autosave, and device sync gives you an instant history and a lightweight workspace model without forcing rigid file structures.

Real World Use Case

A digital marketing team separates client campaigns into spaces so each member opens only relevant tabs during a sprint. When Chrome crashes, autosave recovers the session and team members resume work without reconstructing research links or dashboards.

Pricing

Workona offers Pro from $7 per month and Team from $8 per user per month with Enterprise pricing available on request. The paid tiers unlock broader sync and collaboration features for teams that need consistent cross device behavior.

Website: https://workona.com

Anytype

At a Glance

Anytype is a privacy first collaboration tool that gives you local data ownership, offline access, and peer to peer sync for secure team work. It fits users who want decentralized control over notes, documents, and project data without cloud dependency.

Core Features

Anytype combines private chats, private documents, and private databases into a single app that runs with on device encryption and offline account creation. The product also offers visual content creation and flexible data visualization while syncing between devices via peer to peer connections.

Pros

  • Strong focus on privacy and data ownership: Anytype stores data locally and uses encryption so you keep control of sensitive material rather than relying on centralized servers.

  • Offline and online functionality for flexibility: You can create accounts and work offline, then sync changes across devices when connections are available.

  • Decentralized peer to peer sync for security and control: Synchronization happens directly between devices which reduces reliance on single cloud providers and limits external exposure.

  • User friendly visual and no code content creation tools: The interface supports block style documents and visual layouts that let you build pages without writing code.

  • Open protocols for extensibility and building custom apps: The platform exposes protocols that developers and advanced users can leverage to extend functionality and integrate with bespoke workflows.

Cons

  • Limited remote storage on free plan: The free tier only includes 100 MB of storage which restricts large multimedia projects and extensive archives.

  • Pricing may be a concern for larger teams or extensive data needs: Paid plans start at $4 per month but higher storage tiers and team needs can increase costs quickly.

  • Complexity for users unfamiliar with decentralized systems: Nontechnical users may struggle with peer to peer patterns and local backup expectations compared to familiar cloud apps.

Who It’s For

Anytype is ideal for individuals and organizations that prioritize privacy, data sovereignty, and offline capabilities while collaborating on notes, tasks, and databases. It matches researchers, small security conscious teams, and independent knowledge workers who prefer local control.

Unique Value Proposition

Anytype offers decentralized collaboration where data lives on your devices and syncs privately across your network. That combination of local ownership, offline access, and no central server dependency is the product’s standout advantage for privacy minded users.

Real World Use Case

A research team working on sensitive projects can use Anytype to share documents, manage tasks, and organize datasets across devices without central servers. The team retains full control over backups, encryption keys, and synchronization flows.

Pricing

Anytype provides a free plan with 100 MB storage and paid tiers starting at $4 per month for Plus, $8 per month for Pro, and $16 per month for Ultra, with larger storage and additional features available on higher plans.

Website: https://anytype.io
